# SVG input

## What it is
Parses an SVG with roxmltree, draws it with its own small pixel rasterizer, and puts the raster into a one-page PDF as an image — not as vectors. It is unrelated to the [SVG renderer](svg-renderer.md), which shares the name and goes the other way (PDF → SVG).

## Governing decisions
- [ADR-0001](../../adr/0001-crates-split-by-dependency-layer.md) — `svg` (`roxmltree`) builds without render (#2).
- Units and viewport, decided on #197 (maintainer, 2026-10-07), not built: px and unitless lengths are both 0.75 pt, as CairoSVG does (`dpi=96`, `device_units_per_user_units` 0.75 for PDF); the page is `width`/`height`, with the `viewBox` fitted by `preserveAspectRatio` (default `xMidYMid meet`).

## Design model
Status: read from the source; the units rule is a decision not yet in the code.
- Elements: `rect`, `circle`, `ellipse`, `line`, `polyline`, `polygon`, `path`, `text`, with `g` recursion and `transform` composition (`parse_node`). Paths are stroked as straight segments: curves go straight to their end point ("Simplified"), and paths are never filled. Text becomes a 4 × 8 px marker in the fill colour ("we just place a small marker"); its characters still reach `page_text`. Percentage lengths parse to nothing ("percentages need parent context, skip").
- Size: `parse_svg_dimensions` takes the page from the `viewBox` whenever it has four numbers, ignoring `width`/`height` and the viewBox origin, else from `width`/`height` (default 300 × 150). `parse_length` reads `px`, `pt` and unitless as the same number of points; `in`, `mm`, `cm` convert to points. Measured on #197 (2026-10-07, `justpdf convert`): `width="96px"` and unitless `96` both give a 96 pt page; `width="10in" viewBox="0 0 100 50"` gives 100 × 50 pt. No viewBox → viewport transform is applied to content (inferred on #197).
- Clipping (#452): rect fills and line segments take their transformed coordinates as `f64`. A rect fills `trunc(x)` to `trunc(x) + trunc(w)` (end excluded), computed in `f64` and clamped to the raster. A line is clipped to the raster plus a one-pixel margin (`clip_segment`) before its ends are truncated to `i32` for Bresenham. The margin is there because `as i32` truncates toward zero, so a coordinate in (−1, 0) lands on pixel 0 as it did before. A horizontal or vertical segment is clipped exactly (`clip_span`). Any other segment uses Liang–Barsky on halved differences (`0.5 * x1 - 0.5 * x0`, finite for any finite ends): an end cut by an edge is placed on that edge exactly, its other coordinate is computed, and the segment is dropped when a computed coordinate falls more than one pixel outside the window. Every returned coordinate is clamped to the window, so the walk covers at most the raster's width or height plus two. A shape with a non-finite coordinate draws nothing. The traps, each *measured* 2026-11-01: computing cut ends as `x0 + t * dx` without a clamp left them outside the window at 1e30 (a debug panic, and 6.6 s per line in release); clamping instead of dropping turned `<line x1="1e18" y1="1e18" x2="0" y2="20">`, which passes below a 12 × 8 raster, into a diagonal across it; with a clamp alone, a horizontal line whose far end rounds to `t = 1` (`x1="1e30" x2="-1e13"`) is lost, which is why horizontal and vertical segments take the exact path; and computing the cut coordinate instead of placing it on the edge put `(0.3, -1.3) → (50, 5)`'s start at y = −0.9999999999999999, which truncates to row 0 instead of −1. Where coordinates are so large that their spacing (ulp) exceeds a pixel, from about 1e16, a slanted line inside the raster is drawn approximately or dropped: the review's comparison against an exact clip in rationals lost no segment up to 1e15 and lost about 4% of segments with exponents spread to 308 (*measured* 2026-11-01, a Python port of an earlier version with the same drop rule). A shape whose truncated coordinates lie inside the raster draws the pixels it drew before: `shapes_inside_the_raster_keep_their_pixels` and `a_slanted_line_keeps_its_slope_when_clipped` hold grids recorded from the unclipped rasterizer, and the review's comparison of 2 million random segments found no difference for the 267,258 lying inside (*measured* 2026-11-01). A line cut at the raster's edge is drawn from its clipped ends, not by walking from its truncated ends as before, so it can differ from the old output. On a 12 × 8 raster with about 300,000 random edge-cut segments (coordinates −24 to 36), 25% differ, about 2.3% by more than 2 px, and 1.4% are drawn by only one of the two. Where they differ, the new pixels are closer to the true segment: the largest distance from a drawn pixel's centre to the segment is 1.90 px against 2.24 px before, more than 1 px for 5,327 segments against 14,748, and the new output is further off for 27,071 segments against 42,160 for the old (*measured* 2026-11-01, a probe on `clip_segment`). The maintainer kept the clip on that measurement rather than reproducing the old pixels by skipping the integer walk ahead to the raster (#452, 2026-11-01). They had first accepted "about a pixel at the edge", which the measurement showed was too narrow. Before #452 the coordinates were cast to `i32` first, saturating, and added in `i32`. `<rect x="3e9" width="3e9">` and `<line x1="-3e9" x2="3e9">` panicked in a debug build, and the release CLI walked every off-raster pixel: ten lines to `x2="1e9"` in a 569-byte SVG took 28.3 s and the wrapping line 12.0 s. After the fix each converts in under 0.01 s of CPU (*measured* 2026-11-01, `justpdf convert`). Pinned by:
  - `shapes_reaching_far_outside_the_raster_draw_their_part_inside` and `lines_with_huge_finite_ends_are_clipped_to_the_raster`, each with a one-second bound;
  - `a_huge_line_that_misses_the_raster_draws_nothing` and `a_line_cut_at_an_edge_starts_on_that_edge`;
  - `a_clipped_segment_lies_inside_the_window`: 200,000 random segments with exponents up to 308, an overflowing diagonal, misses, non-finite ends and a subnormal slope;
  - `non_finite_coordinates_draw_nothing`.

  Each of these mutations failed a named test (*measured* 2026-11-01): dropping the exact horizontal or vertical path, the vertical clip, the clip altogether, a finiteness check, the drop-on-doubt (clamping instead), the clamp, the halving, the exact edge placement, `clip_span`'s miss check or the zero-slope guard, or narrowing the margin to 0. Circles, ellipses and polygon fills compute their bounds in `f64`, convert them with a saturating `as i32` and clamp their loops to the raster, so they neither overflow nor walk outside it (inferred).
- `to_pdf` renders at 72 dpi (one pixel per point), drops alpha, embeds the raster as a Flate DeviceRGB image XObject with `embed_rgb`, and draws it over the whole page with `draw_image` (`test_to_pdf_draws_the_raster_as_an_image_object`, #88). Before #88 it called `draw_inline_image` without a `cm`, so the image sat at 1 pt × 1 pt, and pixel bytes holding space + `EI` + space cut the image short.
- justpdf's own renderer draws the result. Measured 2026-10-30 at 88b2a08: a 200 × 100 SVG with a 180 × 80 red rect, converted and rendered back with `justpdf convert`, shows the rect in justpdf and in pdftoppm. Until #229 (closed 2026-10-08) it drew the page blank: the Flate stream was decoded once and `decode_image` decoded it again (measured 2026-09-30: pdfium drew 14400 red pixels, justpdf none; `decode_image` on the once-decoded bytes returned "corrupt deflate stream").
- Input bound: `open` reads the file whole as UTF-8. `render_rgba` sizes its buffer with core `image::rgba_len` ([Image decoding](image-decoding.md)): a raster (declared size × DPI / 72, rounded up) with a side of 0 or more than `i32::MAX` bytes of RGBA is a `FormatError::Format`, so `render_page`, `render_page_png` and `to_pdf` fail and nothing is allocated. Decided on #426 (maintainer, 2026-11-01: the #415/#440 cap, an error rather than a downscaled raster, since the page is the whole document). Before #426 the size was `w * h * 4` in `u32`: a debug build panicked on `width="70000" height="70000"` (measured 2026-10-30), and the release CLI wrapped it to a 2.42 × 10^9-byte buffer, peaking at 4.2 GB for 2.6 s before `embed_rgb` refused the RGB length (40000²: 3.7 GB); after, both fail in under 0.5 s at 1.4 MB peak (*measured* 2026-11-01, `justpdf convert`). Pinned by `a_page_too_large_to_rasterize_is_refused` (70000² overflowing `u32`, 25000² within `u32` but over the cap, and 1000 pt at 7200 dpi); a cap loosened to `u32::MAX` failed it at 25000² (*measured* 2026-11-01). The inner pixel index `(y * w + x) * 4` stays in `u32`, which the cap keeps below `i32::MAX` (inferred). Inside the cap a page still costs its size: about 100 bytes of SVG declaring 23170² asks `to_pdf` for 2.1 GB of RGBA and a 1.6 GB RGB copy (inferred, computed). Nesting: `parse_node` and `collect_text_content` read at most 256 levels below the root (`MAX_XML_DEPTH`), skipping deeper elements and text, and roxmltree 0.21.2 parses without recursing, so a 100,000-deep SVG converts (#449; [XML nesting depth](../invariant/xml-nesting-depth.md)).

## Blast radius
- [Document builder](document-builder.md) — assumes `embed_rgb` takes three bytes per pixel, rows from the top, and that `draw_image` maps the unit square to the page box.
- [Render images](render-images.md) — this is a producer of Flate DeviceRGB image XObjects that justpdf's renderer must decode exactly once; a regression there blanks these pages again (as before #229).
- [Image decoding](image-decoding.md) — assumes `rgba_len` is the RGBA byte length with its `i32::MAX` cap; `render_rgba` allocates what it returns.
- [Format detection](format-detection.md) — `.svgz` is detected as SVG, which this module cannot read (it does not gunzip).

## Known holes / open
- Units and viewport: px read as points, viewBox replaces `width`/`height`, no viewBox transform on content. Tracked: #197
- Curves, fills of paths, text and percentages are approximated or skipped (Design model).
- `.svgz` is not read ([Format detection](format-detection.md)).
- Polygon fill sorts intersections with `partial_cmp(..).unwrap_or(Equal)`, which is not a total order once one is NaN; not reproduced through an SVG. Tracked: #456
